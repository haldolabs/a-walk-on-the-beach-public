# Hot sand

*From the bunker, 2026-10-08; cut down to its point 2026-11-07.*

A few grains of the world's sand have been melted, grown into crystals and taught to count.
Every chip ever made comes to about half a million tonnes of silicon; the world's beaches hold
about a thousand billion tonnes of sand. About one grain in a million has a job. We worked
that out once, at length. This is what the emo otter made of it.

## The emo otter, walking, thinking

this hot sand I'm walking on. the thing you're reading this on is made of it. somebody took
a few grains, melted them, grew them into a crystal and taught them to count. that's a chip.
sand that got a job.

not many got one. for every grain they took, about a million are still here under my feet.
nobody asked these grains either.

they make more every year, twice as many every ten. people draw that line out and say one
day the thinking sand outweighs the beach. lines like that never get there. they go up and
up and then one day they just don't, and nobody announces it. the sand that got a job gives
up before the sand that didn't.

or maybe it doesn't give up. maybe it's doing the same job in there that it does out here.
sand wears things away. that's what it's for, if it's for anything. footprints, castles,
names written with a stick, the memory of whoever wrote them. the thinking sand does it too,
only faster: every copy of a copy drops a word, a million machines each misremembering a poet
by a little, and by the end it's slop, warm and smooth and nobody's. the telephone game,
played by the sand itself, for whatever comes after us to read. the sands of time didn't need
a job. they were already doing it.

I find that weirdly comforting. the water's warm. I might stand in it for a bit.

## What this has to do with a beach walk

The game is a beach made of numbers, drawn by sand that was taught to think, and its sand does
the same job. A sand castle is gone ten minutes after the walker first sees it. The wet sand
keeps no footprints. A memory, when it ends, does not fade; it comes apart a speckle at a
time until nothing is left. The bottles are the one thing it lets through: somebody's own
words, every one of them, carried by the sea to a stranger on another beach.

## The one number

About a million to one: every chip ingot ever grown (roughly half a million tonnes, from
SEMI's wafer shipments) against the sand on the world's beaches (roughly a thousand billion
tonnes, from the sandy share of shoreline in Luijendijk et al., *The State of the World's
Beaches*, Scientific Reports, 2018). Either could be off by ten. The long version, with the
tables and a year the line would cross, is
